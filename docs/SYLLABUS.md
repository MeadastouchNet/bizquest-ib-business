# Syllabus: what each built unit covers

IB Business Management guide, first assessment 2024. Each content point is marked **verified** (checked
against the official guide) or **from memory** (written from knowledge of the guide; check it when the guide is
available). Planned units list their topic titles in `js/content/units.js`; those are from memory too.

## Unit 1: Introduction to business management (built 2026-10-26)

| Topic | Content points | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Introduction to business management | What a business is (combining human, physical and financial resources); the four business functions; primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary sectors; sectoral change; entrepreneurship; reasons for starting a business; problems a new business may face | from memory |
| 1.1 | Intrapreneurship | from memory: may not be named in the 2024 guide |
| 1.2 Types of business entities | Private vs public sector; sole traders; partnerships; privately held companies; publicly held companies; for-profit social enterprises incl. cooperatives; non-profit social enterprises incl. NGOs | from memory |
| 1.3 Business objectives | Vision and mission statements; common objectives (growth, profit, protecting shareholder value, ethical objectives); strategic and tactical objectives; corporate social responsibility (CSR) | from memory |
| 1.3 | SMART objectives; reasons why objectives change | from memory: may not be named in the 2024 guide |
| 1.4 Stakeholders | Internal and external stakeholders; their interests; conflict between stakeholders | from memory |
| 1.4 | Stakeholder mapping (a toolkit item, taught here) | from memory |
| 1.5 Growth and evolution | Internal and external economies and diseconomies of scale; reasons to grow and to stay small; internal (organic) growth; external growth: mergers and acquisitions, takeovers, joint ventures, strategic alliances, franchising | from memory |
| 1.6 Multinational companies (MNCs) | What an MNC is; reasons for becoming an MNC; impact of MNCs on host countries | from memory |

HL: all of Unit 1 is treated as SL and HL (no HL-only parts). From memory.

## Unit 2: Human resource management (built 2026-10-26)

| Topic | Content points | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Introduction to human resource management | Role of HRM; workforce (human resource) planning; labour turnover (formula); internal and external factors that influence HR planning (demographic change, labour mobility, technology, new work patterns incl. teleworking, flexitime, gig economy, laws) | from memory |
| 2.1 | Recruitment (internal/external), training (induction, on-the-job, off-the-job; cognitive and behavioural), appraisal (formative, summative, 360-degree, self), dismissal and redundancy; outsourcing, offshoring and reshoring | from memory: may sit in a different topic, or be reduced, in the 2024 guide |
| 2.1 (HL) | Reasons for resistance to change in the workplace; HR strategies for reducing the impact of change and resistance to change | from memory |
| 2.2 Organizational structure | Delegation, span of control, levels of hierarchy, chain of command, centralization and decentralization, delayering, bureaucracy; flat and tall structures; structures by function, product and region | from memory |
| 2.2 (HL) | Matrix structure; project-based organization | from memory. Handy's shamrock organization is not included (thought to be removed in the 2024 guide) |
| 2.3 Leadership and management | Key functions of management (Fayol); management vs leadership; autocratic, paternalistic, democratic, laissez-faire and situational leadership | from memory |
| 2.4 Motivation and demotivation | Motivation theories: Taylor, Maslow, Herzberg; financial rewards (salary, wages: time and piece rates, commission, profit-related pay, performance-related pay, employee share ownership, fringe payments); non-financial rewards (job enrichment, job enlargement, job rotation, empowerment, purpose, teamwork) | from memory |
| 2.4 (HL) | McClelland's acquired needs; Deci and Ryan's self-determination theory; Adams' equity theory; Vroom's expectancy theory | from memory |
| 2.5 Organizational (corporate) culture (HL) | Meaning and dimensions of culture; types of culture (Handy's four types used as the model); reasons for and consequences of culture clashes; how individuals influence culture and culture influences individuals | from memory |
| 2.6 Communication | Formal and informal communication; methods (written, oral, non-verbal, visual, digital); barriers to communication and how to overcome them | from memory |
| 2.7 Industrial/employee relations (HL) | Employee and employer representatives; sources of conflict; approaches by employees (collective bargaining, work-to-rule, go-slow, overtime ban, strike) and employers (collective bargaining, threats, changes to contracts, lockout, closure); conflict resolution (conciliation, arbitration, employee participation and industrial democracy, no-strike agreement, single-union agreement) | from memory |

HL tagging: 2.1, 2.2 and 2.4 are SL and HL with HL-only parts; 2.5 and 2.7 are HL only. From memory.

| Topic | Content points | Status |
|---|---|---|

| 4.3 Sales forecasting (HL) | Purpose of sales forecasting; three-part and four-part centred moving averages; trend; seasonal, cyclical and random variation; seasonal variation = actual − trend; forecast = projected trend + average seasonal variation; benefits and limitations | from memory: check whether the guide asks for three-part averages, four-part centred averages, or both |
| 4.4 Market research | Purpose of research; primary research (surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation) and secondary research (market analyses, government statistics, academic journals, media); qualitative vs quantitative data; sampling methods (random, stratified, quota, cluster, convenience, snowball); ethical considerations | from memory |

Price: cost-plus (mark-up), penetration, skimming, psychological, loss leader, price discrimination, dynamic and competitive pricing. Promotion: above and below the line, promotional mix, social media marketing, guerrilla marketing. Place: distribution channels, intermediaries, e-commerce. People, processes, physical evidence | from memory |
| 4.5 | BCG matrix (a toolkit item, taught here) | from memory |
| 4.6 International marketing (HL) | Opportunities of international markets; methods of entry (exporting, franchising/licensing, joint ventures, strategic alliances, mergers and acquisitions, direct investment); threats and barriers (cultural, legal, political, economic, practical); standardization vs adaptation, glocalization | from memory |

## The business management toolkit (shown as "Unit 6", built 2026-10-26)

The guide doesn't number the toolkit as a unit; BizQuest gives it id `6` so it gets its own lessons, Boss Battle and
formula sheet. Quiz ids are `6.x-n`.

| Topic | Content points | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 SWOT analysis | Internal strengths and weaknesses, external opportunities and threats; matching them into strategies; limitations | from memory |
| 6.2 STEEPLE analysis | Social, technological, economic, environmental, political, legal and ethical factors; link to SWOT; judging significance; limitations | from memory |
| 6.3 Decision trees | Decision and chance nodes, probabilities, expected value, net gain, rolling back and pruning; strengths and limitations | from memory |
| 6.4 Force field analysis | Lewin's driving and restraining forces, scoring, strategies to strengthen or weaken forces; limitations | from memory |
| 6.5 Gantt charts | Bars over time, dependent (sequential) and parallel (concurrent) tasks, milestones, shortest project time; strengths and limitations | from memory |
| 6.6 Critical path analysis (HL) | Activity-on-arrow network diagrams, nodes with EST and LFT, forward and backward pass, total float = LFT − duration − EST, critical path; strengths and limitations | from memory: free float not taught; check whether the guide asks for it |
| 6.7 Simple linear regression (HL) | Scatter diagrams, independent and dependent variables, correlation (direction, strength, r described but not calculated), line of best fit y = a + bx, interpolation and extrapolation, correlation vs causation | from memory: check whether r or R² must be calculated or only interpreted |
| 6.8 Circular business models | Linear vs circular economy; the five models: circular supplies, resource recovery, product life extension, sharing platforms, product service systems; benefits and challenges | from memory |
| 6.9 The business plan | Purpose of a business plan and its readers (banks, investors, managers); contents (executive summary, business description, market analysis, marketing, operations, HR and financial plans); a worked financial plan (start-up costs, sources of finance, break-even); strengths and limitations | from memory |

HL tagging: 6.6 and 6.7 are HL only; the rest is SL and HL. From memory.

Toolkit items covered elsewhere: stakeholder mapping (1.4), Ansoff matrix (4.2), descriptive statistics (4.4,
