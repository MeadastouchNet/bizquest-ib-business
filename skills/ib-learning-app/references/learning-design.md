# Learning design: why each part of a lesson exists

The learner is a bright teenager with little patience for studying. The design makes the effective thing
the easy thing. Knowing *why* each piece exists lets you write content that actually does the job.

## The research, and where it shows up

| Finding (source) | In the app |
|---|---|
| **Retrieval practice** beats re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013) | Every lesson ends in a quiz; Review is all retrieval. Write questions that make the student *produce* the idea, not recognise a phrase. |
| **Spacing** beats cramming (Cepeda et al. 2006) | Each question goes into a 5-box Leitner system (due after 0, 1, 3, 7, 16 days). Early practice doesn't move a question up a box. |
| **Interleaving** helps pick the right method (Rohrer & Taylor 2007) | Review and Boss Battles mix topics and units. Write questions that stand alone (no "as above"). |
| **Immediate, specific feedback** (Hattie & Timperley 2007) | Every answer shows a `why`. Write the why as the reasoning, not "the answer is B". Missed questions come back at the end of the quiz. |
| **Worked examples, then practice** for novices (Sweller & Cooper 1985; Renkl & Atkinson 2003) | The worked example asks the student to think before each step is revealed; the quiz then has similar problems. Tables with fillable `?` cells make the method visible. |
| **Multimedia principles** (Mayer 2020): words + visuals, short segments, conversational tone | Short cards, live charts in labs, "you" language, read-aloud on every card. |
| **Self-explanation** (Chi et al. 1989) | "Explain it like you're texting a mate" at the end of each lesson; the parent sees the answers in Usage stats. |
| **Personal, relatable context** raises interest and learning (Cordova & Lepper 1996) | A running case per unit with teen characters, plus examples from gaming, music, sport, social media, festivals and part-time jobs. |
| **Autonomy and visible competence** sustain motivation (Deci & Ryan 2000) | XP, levels, streaks, mastery bars, lab challenges, one-tap "Do my 10 minutes". |
| **"Learning styles" matching is a myth** (Pashler et al. 2008) | Everyone gets every format; no "visual learner" modes. |

## Rules of thumb for writing content

- **Hook first.** Start with a situation the student recognises and a question they want answered
  ("You sold your PS5 for $400 but can't buy pizza tonight. Why?"). Then teach.
- **One idea per card**, readable in under a minute, with a concrete example every time.
- **The running case carries the numbers.** Reusing one business (or household, country, team) across a unit
  shows how topics connect and saves the student re-reading set-ups.
- **Ask, don't tell.** Worked-example steps are questions; labs have challenges; quizzes include
  misconception questions ("A favourable variance is always good news: true or false?").
- **No give-away options.** If the right answer is always the longest or most precise option, a clever student
  learns to spot the cue instead of retrieving the idea, and the retrieval practice is lost. Write
  distractors that are as long and plausible as the answer.
- **Exam transfer.** Use the IB command terms and say what earns marks. Mix definition, application,
  calculation and evaluation questions, as the papers do.
- **Accuracy over volume.** A wrong definition or number gets learned as truth. Re-calculate everything and
  keep definitions faithful to the guide.
- **Small and finishable.** About 10 minutes per topic. A lazy-but-smart student finishes short things.

## No video by default

Embedded videos go stale and are watched passively. Labs (doing) and read-aloud (the browser's own speech,
free and offline) cover the same need. Add recorded narration only if Usage stats show read-aloud is popular.

## Evaluate and adapt

The **Usage stats** page shows minutes per day, streak, first-try accuracy and mastery per topic, the
most-missed questions, the student's own explanations, and their one-tap lesson ratings. After a week or
two: are they using it (minutes, days)? Is it sticking (review accuracy on older questions, aim for 70%+)?
Which lessons are rated boring or too hard? Fix those first.
